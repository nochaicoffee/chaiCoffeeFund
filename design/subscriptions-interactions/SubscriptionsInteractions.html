1 · Playing with the map

"What each one costs" now has its hover card. These make the map itself move and answer questions.

A

Morph, don't jump

Switching to By category, each block flows into its category's tile and takes its colour; switching back, they flow out again. A month and A year roll every number to the new figure.

B

Try it without

Hover a block and press Try without: it folds away, the rest grow to fill, and a line says what you'd pay and save. Try several at once. Nothing is saved; it's a what-if.

C

Blocks fill up to renewal

Each block fills from the bottom as its cycle runs, so the ones about to renew are the fullest. One renewing within 3 days breathes softly in its own colour.

Netflix renews in 3 days, so it's breathing. Cult.fit just renewed, so it's nearly empty.

2 · Turning the carousel

The cover flow and the coloured hero under it. These make a pick feel like it lands.

A

Rolling digits

Every number in the hero rolls digit by digit to the new pick's, as on the Cards page's details card, while the colour crossfades.

B

Read the cycle bar

Move along the bar under the name to read the date at any point and how far it is from today. It snaps to today and to the renewal, which shows what it'll charge. Arrow keys work too.

3 · Connecting the pieces

The map, the payments and Charged to show the same subscriptions three ways. These point from one to the others.

A

Point at it

Hovering a payment lights its block in the map and steps the rest back. Hovering a block lights its payments.

B

Light up a card

Hovering a card or an account in Charged to lights its subscriptions in the map, and its bar splits into them with their names.

C

The price change, in its row

Hovering a payment where the price changed opens the row and draws the price over time, step by step, right there.

Hover the Jul 23 row, where Netflix went up.

4 · Small moments

Little things that happen at the right time, so the page says what's going on without being read.

A

A renewal getting close

A cover renewing within 3 days breathes softly in its own colour, faster the day before. On the day it holds a steady ring and says Due today.

B

Paid, with a sweep

Mark paid turns green with a tick, the cycle bar sweeps back to the start of the next cycle, and Paid this year rolls up by the amount. Undo puts it all back.

5 · Reading the day-by-day grid

Over time's Day by day view: every renewal of the year on its day. Today it only has the browser's plain tooltip.

A

A card for each day

Hovering or focusing a day lifts it and shows a light card, like the Cards page's month card: what renewed, where it was charged and whether it's paid. It glides from day to day. A day not recorded says so, and clicking it opens Record a payment.

B

Spotlight a month

Hovering a month's total under the grid keeps that month's days lit and fades the rest. Its card splits the month by subscription and compares it with a usual month.

C

Drag to add up

Press on a day and drag to another: the days between are outlined, and a line adds them up, paid and still to come. Handy for "what does the rest of the year cost?"

6 · Payment trend and Lifetime cost

The trend already has a hover card; Lifetime has nothing to touch yet.

A

A bar splits into its subscriptions

Hovering a month's bar stacks it into its subscriptions, each in its own colour, and lists them beside the chart. The other months step back.

B

A running total

Moving along this year's bars draws a line of everything paid so far, up to the month under the pointer, and says where the year is heading.

C

Lifetime, sorted and opened

Sort Lifetime cost by most paid, longest held or newest, and the rows slide to their new places. Hovering a row opens what it cost each year.

7 · The quieter widgets

The greeting, Charged to, the chips under the map and the toggles everywhere don't respond to anything yet.

A

The greeting points

Each figure in the sentence under the greeting is a link to what it means: hovering it lights the months, the biggest month or the coming renewals it's talking about.

B

Charged to shows the next bill

Hovering a card in Charged to opens which subscriptions land on its next statement, and their total. A bank account shows its next debits.

C

A ghost of what you paused

Hovering a paused or cancelled chip under the map makes room for a dashed ghost block: what it would add each month if you resumed or restarted it.
