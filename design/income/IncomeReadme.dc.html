Income: read me first

Sample data, made up. Today is Oct 24, 2026

The /income dashboard and its Entries page, as designs to pick from. Every dashboard in the app has the same two parts: a main page (the greeting, a hero of its own, widgets) and a page that lists every record (Cards' Statements, Subscriptions' Renewals, Accounts' Balance history). Income gets both. The shell, the greeting whose figures point, the summary tiles, the dropdowns and the ring texture are the other dashboards'; the heroes are new, as each dashboard gets its own idea. Nothing here is built yet.

Round 1, the dashboard: four heroes

Same greeting, same attention strip (pending income is called out, as the PRD asks), and each its own idea for the hero. Under it, widgets built from the same library, arranged differently each time.

I1Payday river
Every source is a lane across the year. A bead is a payment, sized by its amount: solid when it landed, ringed in amber when it landed more than 3 days late, dashed when pending or still to come. Today is a line through the lanes. Pick a lane to follow one source. Light, on the mint feature tile.
I2Money flow
Sources flow into the accounts they land in. A flow map (Sankey): a ribbon as wide as the money it carries, with the pending dividend drawn as a dashed amber ribbon on its way. Pick a source or an account and the rest fades; the facts under it change to that pick's.
I3Income wheel
The year as a wheel, a spoke per month. Each spoke is as tall as what landed in that month, stacked by source; expected months are outlined. Pick a month (the chips round the wheel) and its payments open on the right, with how much of what's expected has landed. On the deep green stage, like Accounts' hero.
I4Year in columns
The plain one, done well. Twelve stacked columns, a usual-month line, a By source / Regular and bonus switch, and chips that light up one source across the year. The figure and its words change with the pick. The most data in the least space.
2

The widgets

Each answers a question about income that none of the other dashboards do. Every one has a hover card, and the switches work without script.

  • Month by month: what landed each month, by source or as regular / bonus / one-off. Pending and expected income is outlined and never added to a total.
  • Sources: the master list (F-IN-01): usual amount, what it's worth a month, received this year, a year of months as a sparkline, the next one, where it lands, and whether it's on track.
  • Coming up: pending first, then what each source's schedule says is next, with Mark received on the pending one.
  • Where it lands: which account each source pays into and how much of the year went to each.
  • Pay rhythm: how many days after its expected date each payment landed, per source. This is why the entry has an expected date and a received date.
  • This year, and last: the running total against the same days of 2025, and which sources grew.
  • Where it comes from, Regular, bonus and one-off: the mix by source, and what a usual month is without the bonuses (the March bonus makes the year lumpy).
  • Pay calendar: the month with each payday on its day.

4

Numbers (definitions)

These are the money rules the mockups use, so every figure on every board agrees. Each needs a yes before it's built, and then a test that proves the math (CLAUDE.md).

  • A month's income is what was received in it, by the date it landed. Pending and expected entries are shown but never counted.
  • A usual month (expected) is the sum of each active source's monthly equivalent: its amount over the months in its cycle (₹1,46,700: salary ₹1,38,500 + FD ₹6,133 + dividends ₹2,067). One-time sources have none. The greeting's "about ₹1,61,880 a month" is different: what was received, over the 10 months so far.
  • Against last year compares Jan 1 to today in both years (₹16,18,800 against ₹14,30,700, 13%).
  • Late is received more than 3 days after it was expected. Pending is expected by today and not received. Upcoming comes from each source's schedule, at its usual amount; nothing is stored until it's received.
  • Change compares a payment with the one before it from the same source and tag; under 2% it isn't shown (Subscriptions' price-change rule).
  • Loan repayments and money moved between your own accounts are never income (PRD).

Open decisions

Each has the default the mockups show. None is answered yet.

1
Which usual month leads
Shown: the sources' usual month (₹1,46,700, when every source pays) in the figures, and the received average in the greeting. Two numbers can confuse; pick one, or keep both with these labels.
2
Late means more than 3 days
Shown: 3 days after the expected date, for every source. Or a number per source (a salary 1 day, a dividend 7).
3
A payment counts in the month it landed
Shown, so a salary that arrives on Mar 31 for April counts in March. Or count it in the month it was expected.
4
New columns on IncomeSources
Shown: color (the shared palette), icon, and the account it usually lands in (so a new entry starts filled in, like Subscriptions' charged-to). Or none, and colour comes from the category.
5
What a tag means
Shown: Regular is anything on a schedule (salary, interest, dividends), Bonus is variable pay on top (the March bonus), Other is a one-off (the tax refund). The bonus is an entry on the Salary source, not a source of its own.
6
Pending entries
Shown: upcoming income is computed and only saved when received, as on Subscriptions. A Pending entry exists only when someone adds one. Or the app writes a Pending entry for every expected payment.
7
RSU and ESPP
Decided 2026-10-24: not income. A vest or an ESPP purchase lands shares, not cash, and ESPP is already taken out of the salary credit, so they live in Investments (source RSU or ESPP). Dividends on those shares are income.
8
Irregular sources
Dividends have a usual amount of ₹6,200 and a usual day, so a missing one shows as pending the day after its usual date. Or sources can be marked Irregular, and never go pending.
