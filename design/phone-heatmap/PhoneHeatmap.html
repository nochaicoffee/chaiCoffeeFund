Design · phone heatmaps · round 1

Heatmaps that fit a phone

Today the heatmap is a table 700 px wide (a year label, twelve months at 46 px minimum, a total column), so on a 390 px phone it sits inside its card and scrolls sideways, and the Overview's five stacked rows do the same. Four ways to make it fit without scrolling. Each is shown on the three cases that matter: Cards and Income (one measure, years down), the Overview with all five measures for a year, and the Overview past a year, one measure over three years. All are drawn at real phone size, with the app's own colours, five shades, no-data stripes, dashed upcoming, ring on this month and the peak. The numbers are made up, except the first months you can see in your screenshots.

Phone width Every phone below re-flows to the width, so you can see the narrowest still fits. C is live: tap a month, step with ‹ ›, or slide a finger across the cells.

My lean: B as the default on every dashboard (it is the same heatmap turned, nothing is lost, and it is the only one that keeps all five Overview measures and all the figures at once), with C as the bolder pick if you would rather see a whole year at a glance. Your call, they are mock-ups.

Where this applies

  • Cards and Income share one heatmap (SpendHeatmap), so one pick covers both. Income's months still to come keep showing what is expected (the upcoming cell can hold a figure; A and B show it, C shows it in the readout).
  • Overview "Month by month" uses the same cell and the same table; it is the one with five rows.
  • Subscriptions day by day already has its own phone layout under 640 px, so it is left alone.
  • On a laptop nothing changes: this is only below the phone breakpoint.
