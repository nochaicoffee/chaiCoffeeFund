Design round 1 · 2026-11-25

Phone navigation and a logo

He doesn't like the phone's navigation bar and says the app looks plain without a logo. Every figure and name on this board is made up. Part 1 has five new ways to get around on a phone, each closed and open, next to today's. Part 2 has five logos, all built from the kulhad and coin of the home-screen icon so the app and its icon agree. The picker below makes every phone on the page wear the logo you like.

Logo on the phones:
Part 1

Phone navigation

Today a phone gets a plain top bar and a menu button in the far corner, which opens a drawer from the left with thirteen sections. Everything is two taps away, up at the top, and the bar says nothing but the app's name. All five alternatives bring the sections closer to a thumb or show where you are. In 1 and 2, four favourites are one tap away and the other nine are behind "More". The four are Overview, Accounts, Cards and Daily; Daily is the hand-kept diary he'll open most. That choice is an open question.

Part 2

The logo

What exists today: the home-screen icon (a dark kulhad with a gold coin on teal, picked 2026-11-19) and a plain bold word "chaiCoffeeFund" inside the app. The icon never appears in the app itself: not in the sidebar, not in the phone's top bar. Each card shows the mark, the wordmark beside it on light, dark and teal, how it holds up from 56 px down to 16 px, and as a home-screen tile and a browser-tab icon.

In the app

The same logo (the one picked above) in the desktop rail, collapsed and expanded, and in the phone's top bar.

What I'd pick

Navigation: 1 (Dock + More). Logo: A (Kulhad) with its tile.

A bottom tab bar is the one thing every phone user already knows, and four favourites become one tap. Anything else sits two taps away, in reach of the thumb. 2 is the same idea with more character. 3 gives the page the most room, with no bar at the bottom. 4 shows every section by name. 5 puts all thirteen under the thumb.

  • Logo A is already the home-screen icon, so one picture runs from the phone's home screen to the app's header. B and E read best at 16 px, D is the cleverest, C is the quietest.
  • Any of these goes in three places: the phone's top bar, the desktop rail (a mark when collapsed, the lockup when expanded) and the sign-in page.
