How to use the app

Chai Coffee Fund

A personal finance dashboard that keeps everything in your own Google Sheet: bank balances, cards, subscriptions, salary, loans, insurance, goals and investments, all on one page each.

Every name, number and date in this guide is made up. It's a demo copy with a pretend person called Rahul, so nothing here is anyone's real money.

A 2½-minute tour of every page, with one spend added on the Daily page to show how saving works.

Your data, your Sheet

The app reads and writes a Google Sheet that you own. Nobody else's data mixes with yours, and you can open the Sheet any time.

Bold figures explain themselves

Each page opens with a sentence. Point at a bold figure (tap it on a phone) to see what it's made of.

Buttons at the top add things

Every page has its own add buttons under the greeting, like Add card or Record income. Saving updates the page at once.

Works on a phone

The bottom bar has Overview, Accounts, Cards and Daily. Everything else is under More.

Overview

Your whole picture: what you're worth today, how the year is going, and what's coming up.

  1. Pick the period at the top right (This year, a month, or a custom range). The whole page follows it.
  2. Each tile is one part of your money. Click a tile to open its page.
  3. Where each month's money went shows every month side by side: income, investing, EMIs, card bills and cash.
  4. Things worth knowing points out what needs a look, like cash sitting idle or a renewal coming up.
  5. Coming up lists the next salary, EMIs, card bills and renewals by date.

Accounts

Every bank and broker balance, and how money moved in and out of each one.

  1. Press Add account once for each bank account.
  2. Press Update balances every so often and type what each account holds today. The chart grows from these.
  3. Pick an account's card to see only that account. Balance history lists every balance you've entered.
  4. Bank statements can be imported from Gmail, so balances fill themselves in.

Cards

Each credit card's bill, due date and spending, month by month.

  1. Press Add card with its limit, statement day and colour.
  2. Each month's bill shows as Pending until you press Mark paid and say which account paid it.
  3. The heatmap shows which months ran high. Statements open the full list.
  4. Sync statements reads card statement emails from Gmail and fills the bills in for you.

Subscriptions

Everything that renews: what it costs a month and a year, and what's due next.

  1. Press Add subscription with its price, how often it renews and what it's charged to.
  2. Swipe through the cards at the top to look at one subscription at a time.
  3. When a renewal is charged, press Record payment. A cancelled one keeps its history.
  4. Renewals lists every renewal with what's been paid and what hasn't.

Income

Salary and every other income, month by month, with pay changes and late payments.

  1. Press Add source for your salary and anything else that pays you, with how often it comes.
  2. Each payday, press Record income, or Mark received on a payment that's due.
  3. A payment that's late shows in yellow at the top. Salary can also be read from bank statements.

Expenses

Where each month's money actually went: card bills, cash and UPI, EMIs, subscriptions and what you invested.

  1. Nothing to type here: it's worked out from your balances, income, card bills and the rest.
  2. Pick a month in the strip to see that month split up.
  3. Press Add a big spend for a large purchase (a phone, a holiday) so you remember why a month ran high.

Daily

A quick diary of everyday spends, with a monthly limit.

  1. Tap a usual spend under Often to add it to today in one tap.
  2. Or type an amount, pick UPI, card or cash, a category if you like, and press Add to today.
  3. Set a monthly limit and limits per category. The calendar shows each day's total.

Loans

Each loan's EMIs, what's still owed, and what a prepayment would save.

  1. Press Add loan with the amount, rate, tenure and first EMI date. The whole EMI schedule is made for you.
  2. Press Mark EMI paid each month.
  3. Add a prepayment to see how many months, or how much interest, it saves.

Lent out

Money you lent to friends or family: what's owed back, with any interest.

  1. Press Add a loan with who, how much, when, and the interest if any.
  2. When money comes back, press Record a repayment.
  3. Interest that's building up is shown day by day.

Insurance

Every policy, who and what it covers, and when it renews.

  1. Press Add policy for health, life, car or home cover.
  2. A renewal coming up shows at the top. Press Renew a policy when you've paid the next premium.
  3. Keep the claims number and notes (waiting periods, what's excluded) with each policy.

Goals

Things you're saving for. Each goal is a planet: the closer to the middle, the sooner it's due.

  1. Press Add goal with the amount, the date, and whether it's a need or a want.
  2. Press Set aside when you put money towards a goal, and say which account it sits in.
  3. A big goal can have parts (a wedding: gift, travel). Mark a goal done when you buy the thing.

Investments

Your holdings at today's prices, returns against the index, and the targets you set.

  1. Press Add trade for each buy or sell. Holdings and returns are worked out from your trades.
  2. Refresh prices fetches the latest prices. US holdings are counted in rupees.
  3. Set your own targets (say 55% India, 15% US) and the page shows how far off you are.

Fixed income

Fixed deposits and PF: what they're worth today and when they mature.

  1. Press Add deposit with the amount, rate and maturity date.
  2. Add your PF passbook with Add EPF statement, and its yearly interest is counted.
  3. Deposits that matured stay in the history with what you got back.

Getting your own copy

Each person runs their own copy: their own Google Sheet, their own Google sign-in, nothing shared. It takes about 30 minutes on free accounts (Google Cloud and Vercel). The app's Setup page walks through every step and checks your settings as you go.

Built for India first: rupees, lakh and crore formatting, and statement imports for Indian banks.
