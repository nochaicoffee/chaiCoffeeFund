Accounts · interactions, round 2

Your picks, taken further: the dock, the wallet, the side pills, and what the card says

You liked the dock, the wallet stack and the side pills that widen, so each has four variations here, and the open card has five new ideas for what it shows. Everything is live: hover, click, use the arrow keys or press Play it for me. Slow-mo shows the choreography; the Accounts switch (3 / 6 / 12) tests each layout with more or fewer accounts. The first round is still on AccountsInteractions.html.

New everywhere: hovering an account in a dock or a pill now shows which bank it is and what it holds before you pick it. Where a card isn't open yet, the hover is how you read it. A stale account (Canara, 73 days) shows an amber dot.

1 · The dock

The same dock, four ways of answering "what is this one?" before you click. Every one glides between items as the pointer moves, and the balance rolls from the last item's to the next.

1

Label chip

A small chip rides above the item under the pointer: bank, account, balance and what changed. It glides from item to item and the digits roll. The quickest to read and the least on screen.

2

Mini card preview

The item opens into a small card in that account's own colour: bank, name, balance, change and its year as a line. The colour crossfades as you move along the dock, so it feels like holding the card up to look at it.

3

Share rings and balances

The dock already says something before you hover: a ring around each monogram shows its share of the total, and its balance is written underneath. Hovering adds the bank, the exact balance, the share and, for a stale one, how long since it was read.

4

Dock on the card's side

A vertical dock down the left edge of the card, which scrolls when it is long, with the label opening to the right. It frees the space under the card, so the line and the figures sit closer.

2 · The wallet

The idea stays: the open card on top, the others as overlapping strips, the line and the figures beside it. Four ways to improve the stack.

1

Lift and reveal

Hovering a strip lifts it and opens a second line (what it's for, when it was read, what changed) while the strips below slide down to make room. Nothing is picked until you click.

2

Upside down

The pile sits above the open card, which is nearest your hand. Picking one pulls a strip out of the pile and the old card goes back in at its place. It keeps the open card low on the page, next to the line.

3

Grouped by purpose

The strips are gathered under Spending, Investment, Emergency fund and Loan payments, with small headings that appear and vanish as cards move. The headings are what makes this one hold up at 12 accounts.

4

Rich strips

Each strip also carries the account's line, its share of the total and its change, for when you want the whole picture from the pile without opening anything. Taller, so it suits fewer accounts.

3 · Side pills that widen

The spines that widen as you hover, in four forms. All four show the bank and the balance on hover, and none of them needs a click to read.

1

Spines that open to a peek

Today's spines, now with a share fill. Hovering widens one to a 236px peek: bank, balance, its year as a line and its change. The neighbours ease aside and the open card gives up the room.

2

Pill column

The other accounts are round pills in a column beside the card. Hover one and it stretches leftwards over the card's edge into a pill with the bank and the balance. It goes to two columns past five accounts.

3

Books on a shelf

The spines stand like books. A hovered one lifts out of the row and writes its balance up the spine beside its name. The quietest of the four: nothing opens until you click.

4

Pill bar on top

A row of pills above the card: the picked account's pill is open with its bank and balance, the others are round monograms that open the same way on hover. The row reshuffles smoothly as the pick changes, and it holds any number.

4 · What the open card says

Five more ideas for the inside of the card, all on the dock with label chips. Each has one idea and one small animation, so none of them is busy. They are drawn at the card's widest, 760px; try Jupiter (nearly empty, so it runs short), Canara (the emergency fund, not read in 73 days) and All.

A

Money through it

A panel shows what came in and what went out this month or over three months, as two bars that grow in, and what is left in the account. Switching the range slides the bars and rolls the three figures. The figures row under the line is no longer needed.

B

The next 30 days

A projected line of the balance with a dot for every EMI, card bill or salary coming up, drawn in left to right. It says in words whether the balance covers them, and shades amber below zero if not. Hovering a dot or a row lights its partner.

C

How long it lasts

The balance as a runway: how many days or months it covers at the pace of recent spending, on a meter with a marker that slides into place. Spend −20% or +20% re-runs it. The emergency fund shows its six-month goal and how far along it is.

D

Drag back through the year

The balance sits above a strip of the year. Drag the knob, or use the arrow keys, and the number rolls to what it was that week with the date and how much it has changed since. Back to now returns it. The one that makes the card feel alive.

E

Update where it's written

Press Update balance and the balance itself becomes the field, in the same large type, with a dashed underline. As you type it says how much that is above or below now. Enter saves: the number rolls, the line says Updated today, and a Saved · Undo chip stays for six seconds. (All has no single balance to edit.)
