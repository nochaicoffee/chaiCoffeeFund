Making the cover move the way the rest of the page does

Everything below is live: click a card, hover, use the arrow keys, or press Play it for me. Turn on Slow-mo at the top to see the choreography frame by frame. The tokens are the app's own. Nothing is saved; the numbers are sample figures.

These are desktop mockups. Widen the window to at least 1000px; on a phone the hero is the picked card full width with the other accounts in a row under it, which is unchanged.

Why it feels jumpy today. The open card and a folded spine are two different components, so a pick unmounts one and mounts the other:
  • the widths snap (only a hover on a spine eases, over 300ms), and the open card's text appears at once;
  • the balance doesn't roll, because the open card is a fresh element each time;
  • the stage colour fades while the cards jump, and the line and the four figures change instantly.
The fix in every option below is the same idea: one element per account that never unmounts. Its width, position and face animate, so everything can ease from where it was to where it's going.

1 · Opening a card

Same spine layout as today, four ways to move between picks. The stage colour fades as it does now in all four; section 2 is about that.

0

As built now (for comparison)

The card you pick opens at once and the old one folds at once. Nothing eases except the stage colour.

A

Glide and settle

The picked card widens while the old one narrows, in one 640ms ease-out with no overshoot. The spine's face fades out first; the open face is already laid out at its full width behind the clip, so nothing reflows, and its parts rise in one after another. The balance rolls from the figure you were just looking at to the new one.

B

The label travels

The vertical name on a spine isn't replaced: it swings upright into the open card's name, and the bank's monogram slides up beside "Your balance". The card still glides, and the name and monogram fold back the same way on the card you leave.

C

Peek, then open

Hovering a spine eases it wider and shows that account's balance and change without picking it, so you can scan the row with the pointer. Click to open it with the glide from A. Arrow keys step through the accounts. A small delay keeps it from flickering as the pointer crosses the row.

D

Spring and ripple

The glide with a touch of overshoot, and the cards move in a wave: the ones nearest the pick go first, the farther ones a beat later. It is livelier than A; the delay is only 40ms a card, so a long row still feels quick.

2 · The stage around the cards

Everything under the cover follows the pick too. Both options use the glide from 1A for the cards.

A

The line and figures follow

The white line morphs into the new account's line (each point eases to its new height), and the four figures roll digit by digit like the balance. The stage colour crossfades, as now. Pick how the line changes below; Jump is what happens today.

B

The colour spills out of the card

Same line and figures, but the new colour grows from the card you picked as a circle across the stage instead of a flat fade. It makes the pick feel like it's the cause of the colour change. About 800ms.

3 · How the accounts are laid out on the cover

Eight ways to show the accounts, all with the glide from 1A and the stage from 2A. The spines hide a balance until you open a card; most of these keep every balance in view, and they differ in how they cope with many accounts. Change the count to see.

Accounts
1

Spines with weight

Today's layout, kept. Each spine now has a fill from the bottom as tall as the account's share of the total, so you can see where the money is before opening anything. It is the quietest change and scales the least (12 spines scroll sideways).

2

A fanned hand

Real cards overlapping like a hand: each shows its monogram, name and compact balance on the visible strip, tilts a little away from the open one and lifts when you hover. Picking one slides the others apart to make room. The strips narrow as accounts are added.

3

Wallet stack

The open card sits on top and the rest are stacked under it as overlapping strips, each with its balance, getting narrower as they go down. Picking one flies it up to the top and drops the old one into the stack. The line and figures move to the right, so the hero stays about the same height.

4

One card and a dock

One big card, and under it a dock of monograms in each bank's colour that magnify as the pointer moves along them and name the account. Cards slide in from the side the account sits on. It is the same idea as the phone layout and holds any number: the dock just gets longer.

5

Ledger rows

A column of slim cards, each with its name, purpose, share bar and balance in line, so the accounts read like a statement. The open one expands in its own place and the rows around it slide away, so the order never changes. The most information at a glance and the tallest.

6

Centred strip

The open card sits in the middle with its neighbours peeking from behind on both sides, smaller and dimmer, each showing its monogram, name and balance on the visible edge. Pick one and the whole strip slides so it lands in the middle. No sideways scroll at any count, because anything more than two away fades out.

7

Card and tile grid

The open card on the left and the other accounts as a grid of tiles on the right, each with its balance. A picked tile grows into the card while the old card shrinks back into the grid, and the rest reshuffle. The grid adds columns as accounts are added.

8

Card and rail

One big card, and the others as a rail of mini cards under it, each with its monogram and balance. The one you pick flies up into the big card and the old one drops into its place in the rail. The mini cards narrow as the count grows, and drop their names when they get too small.

4 · The open card itself

Today's card: five small lines of text, a purpose chip floating in the corner, four equal buttons, and nothing about the account except its balance. Five redesigns, each on the same dock so you can swap accounts (try Canara, which hasn't been read for 73 days, Jupiter, which is nearly empty, and All). They are drawn at the card's widest, 760px; the dock, rail, wallet and ledger layouts give it that room, the hand and the tile grid would need a trimmed version.

A

A bank card you can flip

The account is drawn as a real bank card (chip, last four, name, bank) with the balance beside it. It tilts and catches the light as the pointer moves over it, and flipping it shows the account's details on the back. All is a card showing the split of colours. Actions shrink to Update balance, Edit and a ⋯ menu.

B

The shape of the year

The card's right half is the account's own line. Move along it to read the balance in any week, with the date, or focus it and use the arrow keys. The four buttons become one labelled primary and three icons, which say what they are on hover.

C

Figures inside the card

A glass panel in the card holds a ring showing this account's share of the total (for All, the ring is split into every account in its own colour) and three of the four figures that sit under the line today. If you pick this, the figures row under the line can go and the hero gets shorter.

D

Quiet and large

Almost nothing but the number: a 76px balance, the account's name as a pill, one line for when it was read and what changed, one button and two small ones. The bank's initial sits huge and faint behind it. Close and Delete live in the ⋯ menu, where nobody hits them by accident. A not-read account says so in a chip beside its name.

E

A passbook page

Under the name sits a small ledger of the account's last three recorded balances, with the date, the balance and what changed, so you can see how it got here without leaving the hero. The current balance is on the right. For All, the ledger lists the three biggest accounts with their shares.
