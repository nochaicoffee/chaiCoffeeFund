1 · Spending heatmap

Hovering a month

Today a cell only shows its total. Both options show where the month's money went, how it compares and whether it's paid. The cell lifts and its rings ripple outward under the pointer.

A

Month card

A dark card glides from cell to cell as you move along the row. It shows the total, how it compares with your average, a split by card, and whether the month's bills are paid.

Move along the row: the card slides rather than popping in and out. On a phone, tap a month.

2 · Statements panel

Marking a bill paid from the dashboard

Pending rows get a way to close the bill without opening the Statements page. Paid rows stay as they are.

A

One click, with undo

Hovering a pending row swaps its pill for Mark paid. One click records it as paid in full today, with a toast to undo it. Part payments and other accounts still go through the full Mark paid form.

B

Pay inline

Click a Pending pill and the row opens to quick amount choices: total, minimum or another amount. Paying less keeps the bill pending and shows what's left, the way part payments work now.

3 · Card tiles and details card

Making the hero section feel alive

These can be mixed: switch each one on or off and try it on the tiles. All of them stay still for anyone who's asked their device for reduced motion.

  • A The tile tilts towards the pointer like a real card, with a light that follows it and a glint across the chip.
  • B Selecting a tile fades the details card to that card's colour and counts its numbers to their new values.
  • C A bill due within 3 days breathes gently, so it catches the eye without an alarm.
  • D Utilization bars fill from zero when the page opens.

5 · Hover card colour

A lighter touch for the hover cards

The heatmap and trend hover cards in five colours, each over a few bars so you can see how it sits on the page. They show the trimmed trend card: what was spent and on which card, with the comparisons on the chart itself.

6 · Details card

Making the details card feel alive

Seven effects across the card's three parts: the coloured header, the figures under it and Next due. They can be mixed. Switch each one on or off, pick a card above the details card, and try them. None of them move for anyone who's asked their device for reduced motion.

  • A Move along the limit bar, drag it on a phone, or use the arrow keys to read any point on it. It snaps to what you owe and to your monthly average.
  • B Switching cards rolls each digit to its new value, like a meter, instead of counting up as it does today.
  • C A soft light follows the pointer across the coloured header, and its rings spread a little.
  • D Hovering a figure shows where it comes from. Available lights up the unused part of the limit. The month figures open a small chart of the year with that month picked out.
  • E Hovering a bill in Next due, or its name below, widens it to show the amount and due date. The others step back.
  • F A switch turns the Next due bar into a timeline from today, each bill a dot sized by its amount. The bar morphs between the two.
  • G Marking a bill paid turns it green, then it folds away while the others close up and every total counts down. Try it with the button below.
